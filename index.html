B. Statistical Attack vs. Bounded Discrete Log Search

  • Nature of the Caesar Flaw: The Caesar cipher does not alter relative symbol frequencies. The frequency profile of the plaintext is merely translated cyclically along the alphabet array. Recovery relies purely on information leakage from natural language redundancy.
  • Nature of the Toy ECC Flaw: The vulnerability is not an information leak in the ciphertext, but rather a parameter constraint ($B \ll 2^{256}$). The underlying group operation completely scrambles coordinates, but because $B$ is tiny, brute-force enumeration $j \cdot G$ terminates immediately.
  • Why this Fails against Real-World secp256k1: In production ECC, $d \in [1, 2^{256}]$. The optimal known generic solver is Pollard's $\rho$ algorithm with time complexity $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{n}) \approx 2^{128}$ operations. At a rate of $10^{12}$ operations/second on a massive computing cluster, finding $d$ would require approximately $10^{13}$ years.

C. Intrinsic Vulnerabilities of Basic EC-ElGamal

  • Malleability (Non-IND-CCA2): EC-ElGamal is linearly homomorphic. Given a ciphertext $(C_1, C_2) = (kG, M + kQ)$, an active attacker can forge the encryption of $M + M'$ as $(C_1, C_2 + M')$ without discovering $d$ or $k$. Modern systems resolve this using hybrid encryption: ECIES (combining ECDH with AES-GCM and HMAC).
  • Point Embedding Overhead: Plaintext characters cannot be trivially represented as field integers; they must lie on the curve curve $y^2 \equiv x^3 + 7 \pmod p$, requiring probabilistic algorithms (such as Koblitz's method) which increase message expansion.
