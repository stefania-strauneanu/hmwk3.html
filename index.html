Toy ECC vs. Caesar: Encryption & Cryptanalysis

An operational comparison between classical symmetric monoalphabetic ciphers and group-theoretic asymmetric encryption (EC-ElGamal), investigating statistical frequency attacks ($\chi^2$) and bounded discrete logarithm recovery.

1. Caesar Cipher Implementation

The Caesar cipher operates on the discrete ring $\mathbb{Z}_{26}$. Plaintext letters $x \in \{0, \dots, 25\}$ are shifted with secret key $k \in \mathbb{Z}_{26}$ according to:

E_k(x) = (x + k) \bmod 26   |   D_k(c) = (c - k) \bmod 26

2. Statistical Cryptanalysis via $\chi^2$ Goodness-of-Fit

Because Caesar preserves relative symbol frequencies, an attacker evaluates all 26 candidate decryptions against an expected unigram frequency vector $p = (p_A, \dots, p_Z)$. The correct key minimizes the Pearson $\chi^2$ statistic:

\chi^2(k) = \sum_{i \in \Sigma} \frac{\left(O_i(k) - L \cdot p_i\right)^2}{L \cdot p_i}

5. Comparative Cryptanalysis & Structural Trade-Offs

Characteristic Caesar Cipher Toy ECC (EC-ElGamal)
Cryptographic Paradigm Symmetric Monoalphabetic Substitution Asymmetric Group-Theoretic Key Agreement
Algebraic Structure Additive Ring $\mathbb{Z}_{26}$ Abelian Point Group $\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{F}_{17})$ over $y^2 = x^3 + 7$
Determinism Strictly Deterministic ($1:1$ invariant mapping) Probabilistic (fresh nonce $k$ yields distinct ciphertexts)
Key Space Cardinality $\vert{}\mathcal{K}\vert{} = 26$ Toy: $\vert{}\mathcal{K}\vert{} = 18$ // Production: $\approx 2^{256}$
Information Leakage Preserves natural language letter distribution Completely destroys unigram and n-gram correlations

Key Vulnerability Discrepancies

  • Language Redundancy vs. Parameter Bounds: The Caesar cipher is vulnerable because it fails Shannon's principle of confusion: character distribution is merely permuted. The statistical attack requires sufficient ciphertext length ($L$) to converge. Conversely, the toy ECC attack does not exploit ciphertext distribution; it exploits a truncated parameter space ($B \ll 2^{256}$) where discrete logarithms are computationally reachable.
  • Scalability to Production Parameters: The Caesar cipher cannot be repaired by expanding the key space because $\mathbb{Z}_{26}$ is bounded by alphabet size. In contrast, full secp256k1 operates over a 256-bit scalar field where the best generic solver (Pollard's $\rho$) requires $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{n}) \approx 2^{128}$ operations, rendering brute-force enumeration physically impossible under current thermodynamic limits.
  • Malleability of Basic EC-ElGamal: Basic EC-ElGamal is homomorphic and malleable ($C_2' = C_2 + M'$ decrypts to $M + M'$), failing IND-CCA2 security. Production architectures resolve this by adopting ECIES (combining ECDH ephemeral key exchange with authenticated ciphers like AES-GCM and HMAC).
